# Efua’s Honest Business: manuscript review and revision notes

*Status: revised draft, awaiting review by a Ghanaian educator, a native speaker and a financial-literacy reviewer. Source: `src/lib/story-library/pilot/efua-premium.ts`.*

## What was kept (the things that made it work)

The central message (honesty and trust are worth more than a quick extra profit); Efua’s careful arithmetic; Kwabena as a persuasive friend rather than a villain; Auntie Dede’s "customers are like a garden"; the label on each batch; the growing queue; Kwabena joining the queue; the groundnut cakes; the numbers twelve, eight, forty, twenty-five. Nothing was rewritten for its own sake: the original eight pages became twelve by adding material, and the original lines survive where they were good.

## What was changed, and why

| Issue found in the original | Change | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Opened with a plan but no texture: no place, no senses, no person teaching Efua. | New opening in Nana Ama’s kitchen (roasting groundnuts, golden sugar) with a grandmother who teaches the recipe and the habit of "doing the sums". | Authentic Ghanaian home setting; gives the arithmetic a source of authority; sets up the ending. |
| Kwabena’s shortcut had no cause. | Monday: fourteen of twenty packs sold, six left over, and by Wednesday they have gone soft. Kwabena sees the soft cakes and suggests selling them as fresh. | Makes the temptation realistic and gives learners a numeracy hook (leftovers are a cost). |
| "Use cheaper oil": groundnut cakes of the nkate-cake type are made from roasted groundnuts and sugar, not fried in oil. | Now "fewer groundnuts and a bit more sugar". | Avoids an inaccurate recipe detail. A local reviewer should still confirm. |
| Kwabena promised "twice the profit", never checked. | Efua checks. Cheaper recipe: 20 − 9 = 11 cedis, only 3 more than 8; twice would be 16. | A real critical-thinking moment: test a claim against the numbers. |
| The ending stated the moral aloud ("Honesty is slower, but it lasts longer"). | Kwabena says the extra groundnuts "were never wasted"; Efua replies "That will be one cedi." Final page: she opens a fresh page in her exercise book and begins "carefully and honestly, to do the sums". | Shows rather than tells, adds humour, and echoes Nana Ama. Less moralising for JHS readers. |
| Honest handling of old stock was missing. | Day-old cakes go into a bag marked "Yesterday, half price". | A concrete lesson in honest pricing: selling old stock cheaply and openly is fine; calling it fresh is not. |
| "Eight cedis a day" was stated with no time frame. | "Over five school days, that is forty cedis a week." | Makes the weekly total correct and explicit. |
| Final-month numbers were vague ("selling twenty-five packs a day"). | Kept, with a consistent cost: 65 pesewas per pack (13 cedis for twenty). 25 packs cost 16.25 cedis; profit up to 8.75 cedis, more than the original 8. | The honest recipe can earn as much as the cheap one. Worked answers are in the teacher’s guide. |

## Numbers (all checked by a unit test)

* 20 packs × 1 cedi − 12 cedis = **8** profit; 5 days × 8 = **40** a week.
* Monday: 20 − 14 = **6** left over.
* Cheaper recipe: 20 − 9 = **11** profit = 3 more than 8; "twice" would be 16.
* Honest recipe: 65 pesewas × 20 = **13** cedis (100 pesewas = 1 cedi). 25 packs cost 16.25; profit 8.75 if all sell at 1 cedi; 6.25 if 5 are sold at half price.
* **Prices are illustrative.** A reviewer must confirm they suit the region and year; the guide tells teachers to adjust numbers and answers together.

## Age fit and tone

Reading level proficient (JHS 1 / Primary 6 and above), about 560 words over 12 pages, about 4-5 minutes. Vocabulary: arithmetic, ingredients, profit, leftover, customers, trust, label, honest (all in the glossary). The dilemma is a peer-pressure shortcut with a modest cost, not a crime; Kwabena is not punished.

## Cultural notes and open questions for reviewers

* Efua, Kwabena and Ama are Akan day names (girl born on Friday, boy born on Tuesday, girl born on Saturday). Dede’s language of origin needs confirming. "Nana" is a title of respect; "Auntie" a courtesy title.
* Is "groundnut cake" the right name and recipe for what learners know? (nkate cake and similar sweets).
* Is the school gate selling scenario normal and safe for the intended schools (some schools restrict selling)?
* Are the uniform colours, the market and the home kitchen respectful and recognisable, not stereotyped?
* Does the dilemma avoid moralising for JHS?

## What the learner gets with it

12 pages; 13 activities (profit, leftovers, claim-checking, cost, honest half-price, two vocabulary, an inference, sequencing, a prediction, a critical-thinking reflection, two social-emotional reflections); 8 glossary words; a teacher’s guide with before/while/after reading, discussion, reflection for real life, a worked maths extension with answers, support and stretch, and "check locally" notes.
